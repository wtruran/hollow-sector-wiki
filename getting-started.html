Getting Started

Your first steps in Hollow Sector — a free ship, a pilot, and your first credits.

Starting Conditions

At the start you choose a faction, and your home station is one of theirs. Your home base is where you sell materials, store cargo, and manage your fleet. You begin with:

  • ƒ10,000 in starting credits
  • A free Tier-1 ship waiting in your mailbox — your choice of an SF1000 freighter or an MN1000 mining ship
  • No pilot yet — every ship needs one to fly (see Crew & Pilots)

Recommended First Steps

Step 1 — Claim Your Free Ship

Open your Mail (the envelope at the top). Your faction's welcome message lets you claim one free hull. Take the SF1000 to run cargo contracts, or the MN1000 to work the asteroids near home. It arrives docked at your home station.

Step 2 — Hire a Pilot

Open the Map, tap your home station, and choose Personnel. Hire a pilot from the Flight Crew list, then assign them to your ship. Pilots cost a signing bonus (around ƒ5,000 for a clean one) and match to the job — a Rock Nerd for a miner, a Speed Demon or Evasive pilot for a freighter.

Upkeep & wages. While a ship is on a contract it accrues upkeep, which now includes the pilot's wage. An idle ship costs nothing. If a job doesn't cover its costs you lose money, so check the maintenance estimate before dispatching.

Step 3 — Send It Out

With a crewed freighter, open the Job Board, filter to Freight, and take a short-distance, low-SU contract within your ship's range. With a crewed miner, open the Map, tap an asteroid near your home planet, and dispatch — common ores (Iron, Nickel, Cobalt) carry low risk.

Job ratings run A through F. Early jobs will mostly be D and F rated — that's normal. Focus on net profit, not rating.

Step 4 — Upgrade When You Can Afford It

Once you've banked a little, tap your home station on the Map → Upgrades and build your first one (the Salvage Scanner is a cheap, useful start). From there: buy another ship, hire its pilot, and grow the fleet.

Common Early Mistakes

Accepting jobs beyond your ship's range. Check the range stat before dispatching — ships can't complete routes longer than their listed range.
Ignoring upkeep on long runs. A long haul on a slow ship can cost more in upkeep than the contract pays. Always check the maintenance estimate in the job detail screen.
Selling everything at once. The market has supply depression — selling large volumes of the same material at the same station drives the price toward zero until the daily reset.
