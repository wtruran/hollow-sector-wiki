Wiki / Mechanics

Research

Hire people, pay them every day, and let them work out how to build things nobody else in the sector can.

The R&D Lab is a station upgrade that opens a research programme. You hire Researchers into it, assign them to projects, and each finished project unlocks something permanent. Every unlock is global — researched once, it applies across your whole empire.

Research is entirely optional. Every loop in the game stays fully playable without ever opening the lab. Research makes things better, never possible — with one exception, covered under Production below.

Building the lab

  • Cost: ƒ75,000, bought from the UPGRADES tab like any other station upgrade.
  • Home station only: unlocks are sector-wide, so a second lab would add bookkeeping and nothing else.
  • No reputation gate: the running cost is the real gate.

Researchers and wages

Researchers cost ƒ10,000 each to hire and live at the station. They are not colonists — no food, no water, no health, no sickness. There is no cap on how many you hire, because the wage bill is the limit.

  • Daily bill: ƒ5,000 base plus ƒ2,500 per Researcher, charged every 24 real hours.
  • Concurrency: each project locks the number of Researchers it needs for its full duration. More people means more projects at once — never faster projects.
  • Duration: 4 real hours for the shallowest project, rising to about 24 for the deepest. Headcount is the only other lever.

If you miss a payment

The lab downs tools. It is a stoppage, not a catastrophe:

  • Work already under way is seen through to the end.
  • Nothing new starts until you settle up.
  • Back pay is capped at one day, and the debt does not grow while they are out — a week away costs exactly the same as a night.
  • Nobody quits, no research is ever lost, and no lien is charged.

Ship Upgrades

Nine projects, in order. Each needs the one before it.

  1. Modular Mounts — one module slot on every hull you own.
  2. Expanded Ore Hold — mining module, +25% cargo.
  3. Cargo Extension Frame — freight module, +25% cargo.
  4. Targeting Uplink — fighter module, +4 combat strength.
  5. Auxiliary Mounts — a second module slot on every hull.
  6. Tractor Beam — freight module, pulls cargo out of a derelict.
  7. Scavenger Rig — mining module, strips derelict hulls.
  8. Ablative Armour — freight module, keeps a quarter of the cargo when a run is ambushed.
  9. Ion Thrusters — fighter module, cuts travel time by 20%.

Modules

Research unlocks the type; each module is bought per ship, for credits only. Tap a slot on a docked ship at a station you own.

  • Not inventory: buying installs straight into the slot.
  • Removing sells: you get a third of the price back and the module is gone.
  • Duplicates stack: two Expanded Ore Holds on a two-slot miner both count.
  • Class-locked: a mining module will not fit a freighter.

Production

This is the chain that gates content. The rare end of your industry now opens up through research — everything you could already build stays exactly as it was, and every ship in the game remains buildable from the start.

  1. Platinum / Iridium / Rhodium Refining — the three rare refinery outputs, one project each.
  2. Sensor Fabrication — Sensor Arrays.
  3. Luxury Synthesis — Luxury Commodities.
  4. Circuit Assembly — Electronics.
  5. Instrumentation — Scientific Equipment.
  6. Quantum Lithography — Quantum Processors.
  7. Exotic Compounds — Xenon Thruster Charge and Anomaly Core in a colony Machine Shop.
Locked recipes stay on screen, dimmed and marked RESEARCH REQUIRED, naming the project that opens them. You can always see what you are working toward.
One consequence worth knowing: the Shroud is built with Iridium Core, so an Eclipse pilot needs Iridium Refining before they can build the ship their reputation earned them.

Factions

Seven projects that deepen your standing across the sector. See Factions for what reputation does.

  1. Diplomatic Charter — reputation you lose is cut by 25%.
  2. Trade Liaison — better buying and selling rates at stations held by your highest-reputation faction.
  3. Standing Orders — every faction runs two initiatives at once instead of one.
  4. Registered Manifests — cargo inspections are half as likely to catch you at factions you stand above 300 with.
  5. Letters of Marque — every raider you destroy is worth half again as much, in both credits and standing.
  6. Embassy Program — a tenth of the reputation you earn carries to the rest of the lawful bloc.
  7. Sector Influence — friendly patrols weigh in when a run is jumped at one of their stations.
Letters of Marque and the Eclipse Syndicate pull against each other by design. This chain asks you to pick a side.
