Salvage & Derelicts

Drifting cargo is everywhere in a dead sector — if you have the eyes to spot it and a hold to recover it.

Salvage is free-floating cargo waiting to be recovered. It comes from two sources: the Salvage Scanner upgrade, which strands wreck-cargo near your stations, and piracy, which scatters whatever loot your freighter couldn't haul at a raid's kill site.

The Salvage Scanner

The Salvage Scanner is a cheap station upgrade — ƒ5,000 — that pays for itself quickly. Once installed at a station, it periodically strands a modest cargo drop within 500 WU of that station.

  • Spawn rate: ~75% chance per real hour to strand a new drop.
  • Accrues offline: rolls are replayed when you reopen the app, so drops pile up while you're away (up to the cap).
  • Cap: up to 3 uncollected drops afloat at once — recover them before they fill up and rolls are wasted.
  • Contents: a mix of raw ore and commodities, usually worth a modest 1–3k, rarely more.
Drops are anchored to the orbit of the scanner station's planet — they drift with it rather than sitting at a fixed point, so a drop's exact position moves over time.

Recovering a drop

Tap a salvage marker on the map and send a docked freighter (only freighters can haul it). The panel shows the cargo, its value, the maintenance for the round trip, and how long before the drop disperses.

  • Lifespan: a drop disperses 3 hours after it appears. Reach it before then or it's gone.
  • Range: the freighter must be able to reach the drop's intercept point within its range.
  • One ship per drop: once a freighter is on its way to a drop, that salvage is claimed — you can't send a second ship after the same one.
  • Delivery: recovered cargo is hauled to your nearest owned station and added to its storage.
A salvage run shows a solid trail behind the freighter (the path it has travelled) and a dashed line ahead to the drop — the same way freight routes are drawn.

Raid scatter

When you win a raid and your freighter's hold fills up, the overflow doesn't vanish — it's cut loose at the kill site. If a hull survives the fight the cargo rides inside it (see below); otherwise it drifts as an ordinary drop.

Derelicts

A derelict is a whole wreck rather than loose cargo, and it holds two separate prizes. Your Salvage Scanner turns them up, decoded transmissions point you at them, and every raider or trade fleet you destroy leaves its hulls behind.

  • The cargo still in its hold — recovered by a freighter fitted with a Tractor Beam.
  • The hull itself — stripped by a mining ship fitted with a Scavenger Rig.

Both modules are hard requirements. An unfitted ship cannot be assigned at all.

The order matters. Cutting into a hull vents whatever is still in its hold. That is decided the moment the salvage crew arrives, not when you dispatch them — so a freighter already on its way can still beat them to it. The panel warns you before you commit.

What a hull is worth

A wreck yields a third of the materials the ship was built from, so a tier-3 freighter is worth far more than a starter hull, plus salvage-only goods found nowhere else:

  • Hull Plating — ƒ1,600/CU. The bulk yield; every wreck has it.
  • Damaged Components — ƒ2,600/CU.
  • Intact Avionics — ƒ7,500/CU. A chance per wreck, better on bigger hulls.
  • Xenoalloy — ƒ12,000/CU. Only from the wrecks of alien ships.

Working a wreck

  • Pick what you take: tick the materials you want off the manifest, or leave them all ticked and the hold fills with the most valuable first.
  • More than one trip: a hull too big to strip in one run keeps whatever is left for the next.
  • Risk: a wreck carries a threat rating from the hull that died there — a lone starter fighter is a safe picking, a destroyed fleet is not. Escorts apply as normal.
  • Lifespan: a wreck breaks up after about four hours — but the clock stops while your crews are working it. A wreck you keep coming back to never rots; one you ignore does.
  • Stripped bare: once the hull is empty the marker is gone for good.
Your own losses leave nothing behind. When a ship of yours is destroyed, whoever killed it took the cargo and stripped the hull — there is no wreck to go back for.
