Wiki / Mechanics

Anomalies

Two things that happen to the sector rather than being dispatched by you. One is weather. The other is not.

Anomalies run on their own clocks whether you are playing or away. You cannot start one, and neither can anybody else.

Solar flares

The sun throws off an arc of plasma that expands outward across the sector. Any ship caught inside the band stops dead while its crew brings the drives back up, and writes to tell you.

  • One at a time, rolled each real hour.
  • Speed: it travels at the pace of the fastest hull in the game, so nothing outruns it head-on.
  • Everyone is affected: your ships, ambient trade convoys, and raiders alike.
  • Docked ships are safe. Stations are shielded.

How long you lose

The delay depends on how close to the sun you are caught, and the way a flare looks tells you what it will cost:

  • Close in (≤500 WU): 25 minutes. Bright, tight, and dangerous.
  • Mid-system (4,000 WU): 5 minutes.
  • Out at the belt (5,800 WU): 2 minutes, and by then it is barely visible.
A flare fades as it expands, so a faint wide arch really is almost harmless and a bright narrow one really will cost you twenty-five minutes. The dimness is the readout.

A stalled ship always gets clear — a flare can never catch the same ship twice, because it always travels further than the band is thick during even the shortest stall. Nothing is lost but time: accepted contracts carry no deadline, so a delayed run simply arrives later.

Two side effects

  • The Perihelion drinks it. A solar platform does not stall in a flare — production triples while the wash is over it. The hazard everything else dreads is the one thing that hull was built to sit in.
  • Scanners go blind. A station under a flare loses its Cargo Scanner and Salvage Scanner until it passes.

Alien wormholes

A spiral tear opens in empty space, well away from any station, and holds itself open for two hours. Every fifteen minutes, something comes out.

  • Rolled every three hours. Normally one; rarely, two open at once.
  • Placement: at least 600 WU from any planet, so nothing can drift into it during its life. It sits deliberately between things.
  • It does not orbit — a fixed point, turning slowly, then gone. It leaves no trace.

What comes through

Alien ships, threat 1 to 5, drawn in a purple you will not see anywhere else in the sector. They hunt freighters — yours and everyone else's — and they run down anything slower than they are, which is every freighter in the game.

They only take prey weaker than themselves. An alien compares its own threat against the combined combat strength of a convoy, escorts included, and ignores anything that meets or beats it. A fighter flying alongside a hauler is no longer decoration.

With nothing vulnerable in flight they drift through open space searching, and will turn the moment something soft leaves dock. If one reaches its target, that ship is lost the same way a pirate ambush loses it — and Ablative Armour still saves its share of the cargo.

You are warned as it happens. A tear opening sends a plain notice to your Mail inbox with a button that centres the map on it, and the moment an alien locks onto one of your ships an alert arrives with a button that takes you straight to the contact so you can answer it. Contacts that lock onto passing convoys are left to the convoys; they no longer fill your inbox.

Fighting them

  • Send fighters, exactly like a bounty: each fighter's arrival time is shown, any that cannot catch the contact in time are greyed out, a squadron from several stations converges and strikes together, and the run costs round-trip maintenance shown before you commit. You can also hold and launch a shorter, cheaper intercept once the contact has closed in.
  • Base Defense engages them too, and pays the same bounty.
  • Saving a trade convoy earns +10 reputation with that faction, and a letter from the people you saved.
  • Saving your own ship is its own reward.
  • Every kill leaves a hull carrying Xenoalloy, which exists nowhere else.

Closing the tear

You can send a squadron at the wormhole itself. It rates threat 25 — the best fighter in the game is worth 15, so this needs a real formation and a failed attempt loses the ships.

Close one and every faction in the sector thanks you — including the Eclipse Syndicate. It is the only thing they all agree on. Anything already through is cut off from home and withdraws.

Aliens already in the sector do not vanish when the tear seals on its own. Waiting it out is not a strategy; the threat tails off in its own time.
